# Pixel-specific health comparison under reconstructed 2050 temperatures

12 November 2026 · **Diagnostic only: production, Word, figures and earlier outputs unchanged; not pushed.**

## Main finding and recommendation

Follow-up: the [lower-UHI and regional-versus-pixel sensitivity](uhi_threshold_scale_health_sensitivity.md) retains the citywide Target30 advantage across all five outcomes, while showing substantial UHI sensitivity in absolute benefits. Earlier results below are unchanged.

WorldClim-aligned remains the preferred candidate. Under SSP370 with full cooling transfer, estimated all-cause deaths averted are **38.01 for Green30 and 50.01 for Target30 per modelled June–August summer**, conditional on fixed 2021 population and baseline mortality. The warmer 25/5°C-anchored sensitivity gives **68.49 and 88.75**. WorldClim alignment reduces the ensemble-mean estimates by approximately **44%**, without reversing the aggregate Green–Target ordering.

Target30 exceeds Green30 for every outcome in **every matched GCM, weather pattern, reconstruction, transfer fraction and coefficient-endpoint run**. This is robustness within the tested assumptions, not empirical validation or evidence that LSOA equity associations are unchanged. Do not replace manuscript totals or Figures 6–7 from this citywide comparison alone.

## What changed from the earlier health pilot?

The [earlier five-outcome pilot](cause_specific_response_literature.md#controlled-comparison-completed-five-outcomes-three-scenarios) used a common observed 2021 station baseline across London, with pixel-specific cooling. Its full-transfer all-cause values were 13.27 / 16.54. The present preferred SSP370 values, 38.01 / 50.01, use future monthly climate anchoring **and** pixel-specific baseline temperature contrasts, with five weather patterns rather than one and a slightly smaller common footprint. This is **not a controlled estimate of the effect of spatialisation alone**.

The controlled comparison here is **WorldClim-aligned versus 25/5°C-anchored**, with the same inputs otherwise. Their different absolute temperatures change how often each pixel exceeds the response threshold and whether greening crosses that threshold. The new results also must not be compared directly with annual, unthresholded production totals as though the estimand had remained unchanged.

## Calculation and inputs

Temperature definitions, GCMs, station QC, masks and source paths are authoritative in the [temperature reconstruction report](temperature_reconstruction_comparison.md). No temperature run was repeated or changed. The health calculation reads the saved **baseline lagged daily offsets** and source spatial rasters, then applies each pixel's scenario cooling.

For pixel `i`, day `d`, cause `c`, let `D(i,d,c)` be annual 2021 allocated deaths multiplied by the England cause-specific fraction for that month and divided by its number of days. Let `q(T)=max(T−24.7,0)` and `beta(c)=log(RR(c))`:

```text
avoided(i,d,c) = D(i,d,c) × [1 − exp(beta(c) × (q(Tscenario(i,d)) − q(Tbaseline(i,d))))]
```

Both temperatures are lag-0–1 averages, reconstructed before the threshold is applied. Baseline-hot/scenario-cold pixels contribute only the reduction down to the threshold; pixels cold in both states contribute zero. Counts are summed over pixels and the 92 June–August days. The algorithm uses exact sorted cumulative sums over baseline-hot, threshold-crossing and scenario-hot pixel sets, **not a city-average temperature response or spatial binning**. It preserves pixel temperature–mortality pairing while avoiding massive daily raster storage.

Five all-age responses, thresholds and source provenance are retained unchanged from [the candidate extraction table](manuscript_health_response_candidates.csv), drawn from [Gasparrini et al. (2012)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21389012/). London threshold, England/Wales pooled slopes, regional-to-pixel transfer and June–August subset of the source's June–September season remain explicit assumptions. No new coefficients were fitted or substituted.

**Baseline mortality interpretation:** fixed 2021 seasonal counts are held as the counterfactual baseline mortality under each future temperature reconstruction. This evaluates a conditional intervention comparison. It does **not** estimate how total baseline mortality itself increases with future warming, nor future population growth or ageing. It is not an unconditional forecast of 2050 deaths. This is consistent across the two branches but remains a limitation of their absolute magnitudes.

The common footprint contains **14,020,635 pixels and 8,731,620 residents**. Climate-edge exclusions and no-renormalisation policy carry forward from the temperature test. All five mortality rasters were valid on that exact footprint; none caused additional exclusions. On this footprint, annual / estimated JJA deaths are 56,343.83 / 12,394.84 all-cause, 12,807.96 / 3,015.69 cardiovascular, 4,757.61 / 1,083.45 respiratory, 3,506.29 / 795.09 mental/behavioural, and 432.31 / 113.15 self-harm. These are allocated input counts, not modelled attributable deaths.

## Checks and remaining qualifications

Follow-up: [the coverage and consistency audit](climate_coverage_consistency_audit.md) resolves the climate-induced gap in a separate buffered-input candidate. The results on this page still use the original restricted footprint; no health rerun has been adopted.

Passed: all temperature-input hashes unchanged; exact temperature footprint and population denominator reproduced; nonnegative cooling and valid mortality grids; 4,500 unique result combinations; baseline avoided deaths zero; full-transfer benefits at least half-transfer; monotone RR endpoints; anchored benefits at least aligned; all Target30–Green30 comparisons checked. Direct calculations on 1,500 reproducibly sampled real pixels, including cold/hot and threshold-crossing test offsets, agree with the cumulative algorithm to **1.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ deaths or better**. Post-run hashes of health inputs also verified. These establish computational consistency, not empirical model validity.

**Assessment: suitable for review as a conditional diagnostic, not automatic production replacement.**

- The InVEST 5°C UHI and spatial pattern are not independently validated as daily Tmax. WorldClim alignment corrects the monthly anchor, not these structural assumptions.
- Regional response slopes/thresholds applied to pixels may not reproduce the estimand of the original regional epidemiology. Fine-grid output should not imply equally fine-scale validation.
- England monthly occurrence fractions remain a proxy for London borough registration-month cause counts; baseline daily deaths are assumed uniform within each month.
- Future variability, adaptation, demographic change and age-specific responses are not estimated. Historic weather patterns are centred by year/month, not full future climate simulations.
- Common climate coverage omits 1.67% of otherwise valid UCM cells and 1.16% of population. Resolve buffered climate coverage or retain this restriction explicitly before manuscript production.
- Outputs are exact sums of pixel-level effects, **not new health maps or LSOA equity curves**. Figure 6/7 conclusions require their own downstream reassessment if the approach is adopted.
