Revised Abstract — draft for review

Urban greening can reduce heat exposure, but its health benefits may be unevenly distributed. We combined land-use scenarios, climate projections and spatial models of cooling, mortality, energy costs and heavy-work capacity across London. Under identical mid-century climate conditions, Green and Target scenarios averted 135–496 modelled annual deaths, holding 2021 population and mortality fixed. Avoided energy costs reached £22.4 million per month, and area-weighted heavy-work capacity increased by up to 1.42 percentage points. Benefits under proximity-based greening were generally lower in neighbourhoods with greater income deprivation and ethnic-minority population shares. Compared with Green30, Target30 reduced the share of high-SVI neighbourhoods receiving relatively low health benefits from 45.5% to 4.5%, but increased that share in neighbourhoods with older populations. These findings suggest that planting location shapes both aggregate benefits and equity trade-offs, requiring explicit choices between vulnerability priorities.

This draft omits the unverified temperature extrema and emphasizes the Green/Target comparisons. It does not imply demographic projections to 2050.

Figure 4 Results — proposed replacement

Across Green and Target scenarios, modelled annual heat-related deaths averted ranged from 135 to 496, with larger citywide benefits under Target at each canopy tier across all three outcomes (Fig. 4). Target30 averted 496 deaths (95% exposure–response uncertainty interval, 347–640), compared with 387 (272–500) under Green30. Their respective avoided energy costs were £22.4 million and £16.3 million per month, and area-weighted heavy-work capacity gains were 1.42 and 1.19 percentage points. Conversely, AllBuilt resulted in 1,110 additional annual deaths, £49.6 million higher monthly energy costs and a 6.36-percentage-point capacity loss. These comparisons isolate land-use effects under identical mid-century climate conditions, holding 2021 population and mortality fixed.

Figure 5 Results — proposed replacement

Citywide gains masked substantial borough-level variation (Fig. 5). Under Target30, annual deaths averted ranged from 0.29 to 29.75 per borough, avoided energy costs from £0.08 million to £2.53 million per month, and heavy-work capacity gains from 0.30 to 3.61 percentage points. The largest gains occurred in different boroughs: Newham for health, Hillingdon for energy and Barking and Dagenham for work capacity. Although Target30 exceeded Green30 citywide for all three outcomes, it delivered greater health and energy benefits in 22 of 33 boroughs for each outcome, and greater work-capacity gains in 21. Targeting therefore increased aggregate benefits without improving every borough’s outcomes relative to Green30.

Verification: calculated directly from the 891 rows (33 boroughs × nine scenarios × three outcomes) of figure5_borough_cobenefits_20261110_v3_data.csv (numerically unchanged from v2). The sets of 22 boroughs need not be identical across outcomes. Decimal deaths are modelled expectations, not observed counts. Counts compare point estimates, not statistically significant differences.
