Matched 2050 temperature sensitivity

Follow-up: the observation-anchored UCM climate-parameter pilot is the current decision document. This mean-matching test remains a sensitivity, not the recommended method for physically defining T_ref and UHI_max.

Temperature-only diagnostic, 11 November 2026. Existing production outputs and manuscript figures are unchanged. Health, work capacity and energy are on hold.

Decision-relevant result

Matching Figure 1’s 2041–2060 London mean lowers absolute temperatures relative to the existing 25/5°C configuration, but does not change land-use cooling contrasts or their ranking when UHI and all other cooling inputs remain fixed. This is mean matching, not independent validation or a newly executed InVEST run.

The fitted reference is an effective model parameter, not an observed rural temperature. Do not interpret its numerical proximity to 22°C as evidence that regional climate does not warm: the starting 25/5°C configuration was already warmer than the target London mean.

Assumptions and checks

  • Targets are the equal-weight means of 33 boroughs and three GCMs in Figure 1, separately for SSP370 and SSP585, for July mean daily maximum temperature in 2041–2060. The SSPs are not pooled.
  • Match the baseline land-use configuration only. Apply the same shift to all nine alternative landscapes; independently fitting each landscape would erase the effects being evaluated.
  • Retain UHI = 5°C, current land cover, ET, cooling method, mixing parameters, grid and London mask. The suitability of 5°C as a July air-UHI parameter is not established by this test.
  • InVEST 3.20.2 uses T_ref + (1 − HM) × UHI_max before normalized mixing. A constant reference-temperature change therefore translates the field. This was checked against executed 25/5 and 28/5 rasters for all ten landscapes: the largest deviation from a uniform +3°C shift was 0.000017524°C over 15,733,956 common London cells. Relevant land-cover and cooling arguments agree between each paired run.
  • Calculation: shift = Figure 1 target − existing baseline borough mean; matched T_ref = 25 + shift; matched temperature = existing temperature + shift. No temperature rasters were overwritten or new full model runs undertaken.
  • The diagnostic exports both equal-borough and area-weighted means. Only the first is calibrated, because it matches Figure 1’s aggregation. The matched area-weighted baseline means are 25.6657°C and 26.0122°C, respectively.
  • Every borough’s scenario-minus-baseline difference is unchanged to numerical precision. Spatial gradients and within-landscape temperature spread are also unchanged by the translation; the Figure 1 spatial field is not reproduced.

These unchanged contrasts are conditional on fixed UHI and cooling inputs, not proof that cooling benefits are insensitive to all climate assumptions. No conclusion about unchanged health or work-capacity benefits follows: those components can respond nonlinearly to absolute temperatures and were not tested.

Interpretation and next decision

The existing 25/5°C setting is a warmer thermal scenario than Figure 1’s 2050 ensemble means. Mean matching offers a transparent sensitivity alternative, but neither validates the UHI magnitude nor solves the rural-reference, temperature-variable and spatial-resolution questions. Decide whether to retain 25/5°C as a warm scenario or adopt a climate-matched configuration only after reviewing those assumptions. Downstream tests remain explicitly on hold.
